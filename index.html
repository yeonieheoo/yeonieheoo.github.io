CASE STUDY 01

Demand Forecasting
for Global Catering Operations

ByteDance · 2024–2025 Python · Hive · Aeolus
92.2%accuracy (WMAPE 7.8%)
18workplaces live
5.8%monthly cost reduction (pilot)
The Business Problem

Predicting daily catering demand across global workplaces of ByteDance was historically inaccurate, waste rates ranging up to ~40% a day in certain region. Fluctuating RTO rates, last-minute travel, weather, pollution, seasonality, and elongating holiday impact led to substantial food waste when over-ordered and operational shortages when under-ordered.

Operations relied on intuition and weekly manual adjustments, spending several weeks to 1 month to set up offline spreadsheet models in addition to 1-3 hours weekly searching for the right data and updating these offline spreadsheets. The team needed a model that could handle real-world chaos in a timely and a more reliable manner to scale and standardize forecasting data and strategies.

  • 📉 Inconsistent accuracy across 18 global hubs
  • 🍱 Significant food waste in over-forecast scenarios
  • ⏱ 1–3 hours of weekly manual forecasting per workplace
  • 🌍 Two calendar systems (Lunar + Gregorian) creating holiday mapping chaos
Strategy & Logic

The Hypothesis

Manual forecasts failed because they treated attendance as one monolithic metric. My hypothesis: by decoupling attendance into 6 granular components — and recognizing hidden patterns in RTO fulfillment, weekdays, holiday impact, and leadership travel schedules - we could drastically improve precision.

Why WMAPE, not MAPE

Standard MAPE disproportionately penalizes small workplaces (e.g., a miss of 15 people in Hong Kong reads as 30% error). WMAPE anchors evaluation in actual cost and volume — prioritizing accuracy where the highest budget is at stake.

Stakeholder alignment

A model only matters if Operations trusts it. I built a dashboard that not only outputs the forecast but breaks down why (baseline vs. traveler vs. holiday adjustments), and embedded a system flag that alerts on accuracy drops so Ops and I iterate on bottlenecks together. This cycle of close check-ins helped the users better understand the underlying foundation of the model and allowed me to increase the accuracy even further.

Technical Deep Dive

Predictive modeling by component

Smoothing
Base RTO & Dining Rate

4-week moving average per weekday (e.g., predicting Tuesday from last 4 Tuesdays), strictly excluding holidays and statistical anomalies.

Rolling Regression
Travel & Leaves

90-day rolling linear regression mapping D-x advance requests to realized attendance. Predicts travel volume up to 14 days out from leading indicators.

EDA Insight
The "Leadership Effect"

EDA revealed L3 Department Head travel triggers hidden accompaniment surges. I bifurcated inbound regression into Trips w/ Dept Head vs. Trips w/o Dept Head — improved accuracy ~20% on leadership days.

Iterative
Net Boarding Propagation

Iterative carry-over algorithm propagates new joiners (+) and resignations (−) into future dates as a rolling tally adjusting every forecast.

Algorithmic edge cases

  • Spike filtering: Mean ± 1σ over 90 days flags spikes; 3 consecutive spike days promote to "new trend" and remove the anomaly tag.
  • Cross-year holiday regression: Maps (D-3 to D+2) fulfillment curve to prior-year equivalent, with geo-fallback (exact date → 15-day window → workplace → city → country) to handle shifting Lunar dates.
  • RTO plan-change smoothing: Detects >50% policy shifts and applies 4-week transitional smoothing to prevent over-reliance on obsolete data.
  • Underestimation auto-correction: Misses >3% trigger gap-add to the next matching weekday — captures seasonal shifts faster than monthly macros.

The model didn't just hit a number. It changed how Operations works and thinks — from gut-call ordering to data-supported decision-making, with manual override kept in human hands for genuine edge cases and last minute changes.

CASE STUDY 02

Cost Savings & Compliance via
User Travel Behavior Analytics

ByteDance · 2024–2025 XGBoost · SHAP · SQL · Python
¥2Mannual savings identified
5.2%domestic flight cost reduction
6anomaly vectors engineered
The Business Problem

Corporate travel expenses were scaling unsustainably with global business expansion. The company lacked visibility into two major cost drains: suboptimal booking habits (last-minute flights) and non-compliant / wasteful behaviors (weekend getaways disguised as business trips).

Finance was applying blunt-instrument policies — "spend less" mandates — without data to back which behaviors actually drove cost. Product wanted to ship cost-saving features but didn't know which user behavior to nudge. I needed to quantify how people travel, not just how much and provide insights for the next product initiatives and corporate policy changes.

Strategy & Logic

The Hypothesis

Travel costs weren't rising because of volume — they were rising because of behavior: last-minute bookings, weekend extensions, repeat trips. Quantifying these would shift the conversation from "please spend less" to targeted, systemic restrictions Finance and Product could actually ship.

Why SHAP over regression?

Linear regression gives correlations, not magnitudes a stakeholder can ship. SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) let me say, unambiguously: "Booking 0 days ahead adds ¥X. Booking 4 days ahead removes ¥Y." That's the kind of statement Product could turn into a feature.

Cost proportion, not trip count

When flagging anomalies, I tracked percentage of total department cost the trips represented — not raw counts. 6.1% of trips being "weekend getaways" matters less than the 2.5% of total budget they consumed. That re-prioritized everything Finance flagged.

Technical Deep Dive

ML-driven cost driver modeling

Trained XGBoost / LightGBM on 2024 global flight + hotel data, then applied SHAP to mathematically isolate the marginal financial impact of each booking behavior.

Feature Engineering
Mixed continuous + binary

flight_lead_time, hotel_lead_time continuous + one-hot encoded departure_weekday_5, airline_name_*, class_name_en_BUSINESS.

SHAP Mapping
Feature → financial impact

High flight_lead_time clustered in the negative SHAP zone (cost-reducing). departure_weekday_5 + specific airlines drove consistent positive SHAP (cost-increasing).

Strategic Insight
Diminishing-returns inflection

I plotted cumulative savings vs. lead time and found a clear inflection at 4 days. Booking >20 days out showed no statistically meaningful additional savings — so the in-product nudge targeted 4 days, not "as early as possible."

Geospatial Segmentation
The Mega-City Paradox

Corporate vs. OTA hotel pricing inverted by city tier. Mega cities (Beijing, Shanghai) → Corporate cheaper. Tier-2 (Chengdu, Hangzhou) → OTA undercut Corporate. Prevented a blanket "always book Corporate" policy that would've cost money.

Scenario-based anomaly engine — six vectors

A
Weekend Getaway Logic

Check-in/out vs. business days. Sun/Mon check-outs (10.7%), Fri/Sat check-ins (9.72%). Weekend-only = "getaway" (2.5% of hotel cost).

B
Geospatial Outliers

Geocoord distance from hotel → nearest office. >30km flag = high-risk non-business (8% of orders, 4% of cost).

C
Long-Stay & Cumulative Visits

Group by Employee_ID × City. Trips >25 days (2.1% of cost). >5 visits/yr or >100 cumulative days (26% of spend) → relocation ROI review.

D
Route-Average Fare Audit

Moving avg cost per route. Tickets >50% above route avg flagged (22% of flight cost).

E
Hotel Hopping

SQL LAG/LEAD windows detect check-out matching check-in at a different hotel, same city. Flag → investigate.

F
Leave Day Audits

Cross-ref travel dates with HR leave system → flag corporate-paid hotels during personal PTO.

Strategic Impact
¥55.3M
Annual savings identified

Domestic flight cost reduction from shifting average booking lead time 0 → 4 days.

1 feature
Shipped in product

Insights drove a new in-product advance-booking savings prompt — direct path from SHAP value to UI.

3 teams
Stakeholders activated

Finance overhauled policy. Product shipped UX. IMS pursued compliance follow-ups on flagged individuals.

With cost drivers and anomaly vectors isolated, I built simulation functions to pre-quantify policy hypotheticals — projecting realized savings across both lead time and abnormal behavior levers before Finance committed. Each policy went into stakeholder meetings tagged with an ROI estimate, not a hunch.

CASE STUDY 03

ML-Driven RFM Cohort &
Segmentation Analytics

TikTok Product Strategy & Ops · 2023–2024 scikit-learn · K-Means · Pandas · Aeolus BI
80%manual analysis eliminated
M0–M6cohort heatmaps automated
2+ teamsexpansion (Growth, SMB)
The Business Problem

The Product Strategy & Operations (PSO) and Sales teams were tracking client evolution and revenue opportunities manually — pulling per-account data, eyeballing patterns, writing case studies. It didn't scale, and successful activation tactics weren't transferable across verticals.

I needed a framework that could segment thousands of advertisers behaviorally — not just by spend — and surface upsell and churn signals fast enough for Sales to act on. And critically: I needed the framework to live where Sales already worked, not in a Jupyter notebook I owned.

Strategy & Logic

Why RFM over deep-learning propensity

Complex propensity / LTV models offer higher predictive accuracy but operate as black boxes that frontline Sales teams won't trust. RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) is the bridge: behavior-driven, inherently interpretable, and when paired with unsupervised clustering, it removes arbitrary human thresholds.

Instead of guessing "active 5x a month = good," K-Means dynamically defined the natural boundaries of advertiser behavior, surfacing segments like:

  • High-Value / Low-Frequency → immediate churn risk, retention plays needed
  • High-Frequency / Low-Monetary → primed for product upsell
  • Low-Frequency / Low-Monetary → reactivation candidates

Why M0–M6 cohorts?

Point-in-time metrics hide lifecycle dynamics. By tracking the 6-month retention decay curve after a sales activation, I shifted the team from "is this account healthy today?" to "is this account on a healthy trajectory?"

Scalability as a strategic decision

The most important call I made on this project wasn't technical — it was deciding to migrate the entire pipeline from Python to Aeolus BI. A model isolated from the people driving revenue is a model that dies. Self-serve architecture was the difference between "data science experiment" and "enterprise tool."

Technical Deep Dive

Data engineering & preprocessing

Signal Isolation
Strip ghost activity

Filtered out non-revenue-generating days (Revenue per Day != 0) so frequency and monetary calculations reflected only true monetized engagement.

Temporal Coercion
Unified timeline

Standardized erratic timestamps — p_date, activation_date coerced into clean datetime — establishing a single observation period.

Programmatic RFM feature engineering

R
Recency

Days since the advertiser's last active spend. Captures immediate churn signal.

F
Frequency

Ratio of active days within observation window. Captures platform stickiness.

M
Monetary

Revenue per Day ÷ Total Ad Spend. Captures financial scale and ROI.

Unsupervised ML clustering

Deployed K-Means via scikit-learn across engineered RFM features. Because Monetary skews distance metrics (larger numeric scale than Recency/Frequency), I applied feature scaling/normalization before clustering — ensuring the algorithm grouped clients by holistic behavior, not just total spend.

Automated multi-dimensional cohort matrix

Tracking client evolution manually was error-prone because "Month 1" shifts depending on acquisition month. I engineered a rolling time-window matrix:

  • Dynamic Lifecycle Mapping: rows = lifecycle stages (M0–M6), columns = acquisition cohorts (2022-01 → 2022-12)
  • Time-Shift Algorithm: M1_months array programmatically aligned each cohort — 2022-01's M1 evaluated in Feb, 2022-02's M1 evaluated in March, etc.
  • Converted diagonal staggered raw data into a clean horizontal heatmap — instantly readable retention decay

From Python to production

Reverse-engineered the Python logic into Aeolus high-level queries. Parameterized vertical + product filters so Growth and SMB managers can generate RFM clusters and M0–M6 heatmaps on-demand for their own portfolios.

80%
Manual analysis eliminated

What was a 1–2 day pull-and-eyeball became a parameterized dashboard query.

The strategic lesson: a model's value is bounded by who can use it. Migrating from Python to BI was where this project went from "interesting analysis" to "operating system for client growth."
